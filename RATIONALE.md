# OFL — Rationale

## 1. Purpose

The Onyx Freedom License (OFL) is an experimental attempt to maximize software freedom through a simple, permissive licensing model.

Its central principle is that recipients should have as much freedom as legally possible to use, modify, redistribute, and incorporate software into their own projects.

## 2. No Copyleft

OFL does not aim to enforce a particular licensing model on derivative works.

Recipients may distribute modified versions under permissive, copyleft, proprietary, or other terms, subject to applicable law and rights they actually possess.

## 3. No Source-Sharing Obligation

Recipients are not required by OFL to publish source code or disclose private modifications.

This allows individuals and organizations to use the software in both public and private projects.

## 4. Minimal Attribution Requirements

OFL aims to avoid mandatory attribution and license-notice preservation requirements for redistribution, to the extent legally permitted.

Authors may still choose to provide attribution voluntarily.

## 5. Patent Considerations

The draft includes a limited patent grant intended to reduce uncertainty around patents held by contributors.

Patent provisions require careful legal review because their interpretation and enforceability can vary by jurisdiction.

## 6. Legal Boundaries

No license can grant rights that its author does not possess or override all mandatory legal requirements.

OFL therefore acknowledges third-party rights and applicable legal limitations.

## 7. Current Limitations

OFL is experimental. Its wording, patent provisions, copyright treatment, and interaction with third-party contributions require further review.

The project should not be treated as a legally validated alternative to established licenses until that review has taken place.

## 8. Guiding Principle

**Freedom for the recipient, without a requirement to share modifications.**
